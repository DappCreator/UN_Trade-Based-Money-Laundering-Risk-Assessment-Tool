# Annex IV: Glossary and Annex V: Blank Assessment Working Paper

## Purpose

Annex IV defines terms used in the Trade-Based Money-Laundering (TBML) Risk Assessment Tool. Annex V provides a blank working-paper template for recording the evidence, reasoning, ratings and deficiencies identified for each assessed input variable.

## Glossary

### Competent authorities

For “reporting intermediary”, competent authorities are public authorities with designated responsibilities for combating money-laundering and/or terrorist financing. They include the FIU; authorities investigating or prosecuting money-laundering, predicate offences and terrorist financing; authorities freezing, seizing or confiscating criminal assets; authorities receiving cross-border currency and bearer-negotiable-instrument reports; and AML/CFT supervisors or monitoring authorities. Self-regulatory bodies are not competent authorities for this purpose.

For “free trade zone” and “trade facilitator”, competent authorities include public authorities such as police, customs, fiscal and market-surveillance officials with jurisdiction over relevant people, places, processes or goods.

### Free trade zone (FTZ)

An area designated by a country or jurisdiction where goods entering the area are not subject to, or are subject to lower, import or export duties than those that would apply if the goods were released for free circulation when they entered. Facilities used only for temporary storage or customs warehousing are not FTZs.

### FTZ management

A person, group of persons or entity managing an FTZ on behalf of its owner, including through delegation, outsourcing or contracting.

### FTZ economic operator

An operator working or operating within an FTZ, licensed by the regulator and paying for premises or services provided by the FTZ, such as security, logistics or handling.

### Reporting intermediary

A natural or legal person acting as a middleman between principal trading parties, such as an exporter and importer, that falls within the jurisdiction’s AML/CFT obligations as a financial institution or designated non-financial business or profession.

### Trade facilitator

A natural or legal person acting as a middleman between principal trading parties that does not fall within the jurisdiction’s specific AML/CFT obligations for financial institutions or designated non-financial businesses and professions.

## Functional overlap among trade facilitators

Trade facilitators may perform overlapping functions. Freight forwarders, consolidators, warehouse operators and other actors should therefore be assessed by what they actually do with goods, trade documents and, where relevant, financial settlement, rather than by formal labels alone.

A function-based approach helps identify the actors that create TBML exposure and ensures that risk analysis reflects the practical structure of the supply chain.

## Indicative trade-facilitator types

The list below is non-exhaustive.

| Type | Core functions | Documentation handling | Commodity handling | Potential TBML or criminal risk |
|---|---|:---:|:---:|---|
| Freight forwarders | Arrange door-to-door shipping, storage, customs clearance and documentation | Yes | Yes | Invoice, bill-of-lading, consolidation or cargo-description manipulation |
| Shipping companies | Operate vessels or aircraft, issue bills of lading and cargo manifests | Yes | Yes | Cargo substitution and falsified weight, origin, destination or manifest data |
| Shipping agents | Coordinate carriers, ports, berthing, documents and cargo operations | Yes | Yes | Manipulated shipping documents or substituted container contents |
| Non-vessel operating common carriers | Book cargo space, issue house bills of lading and consolidate shipments | Yes | Yes | Misdeclared shipments or fraudulent bills of lading |
| Port operators | Run terminals and manage loading, unloading, equipment and logistics | No | Yes | Concealment, swapping, diversion, misplacement or misrouting of goods |
| Customs brokers | Submit customs declarations for exporters or importers | Yes | No | Falsified declarations |
| Freight insurance providers, unless reporting intermediaries | Assess shipment risks and provide cargo insurance | Yes | No | Misrepresented cargo values or payments for non-existent goods |
| Warehouse operators | Store goods, manage inventories and support clearance and inspections | Yes | Yes | Manipulated inventory, insurance records or customs declarations |
| Inland container depot operators | Provide inland storage, handling and customs-related services | Yes | Yes | Altered documents, cargo substitution, changed routing or misdeclaration |
| Transport companies | Move, consolidate, cross-dock and store cargo by road, rail or inland waterway | No | Yes | Cargo switching, false packaging or concealment in bulk cargo |
| Export houses | Find international buyers and manage logistics, documentation, customs and sometimes financing | Yes | Yes | Invoice, quality or double-invoicing manipulation, depending on services |
| Inspection companies | Inspect shipments, cargo and vessels and verify quality, safety or compliance | Yes | Yes | False reports that legitimize fictitious or fraudulent shipments |

The documentation-handling and commodity-handling classifications are indicative and should be adapted to local functions.

## Documentation principles

The working paper should make each score reproducible and transparent. Record who participated, what information was considered, why the rating was selected, what limitations affect confidence and which gaps should be addressed through the action plan.
