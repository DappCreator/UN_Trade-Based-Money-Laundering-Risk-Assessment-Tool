# Optional Module for Comparative Risk Analysis of Free Trade Zones (Module 4)

## Purpose and use

Module 4 is an optional component of the UNODC–World Bank Trade-Based Money-Laundering Risk Assessment Tool. It supports comparative risk analysis and ranking of multiple free trade zones (FTZs) within a jurisdiction.

It is especially useful for jurisdictions overseeing several FTZs because it provides a more granular view of relative risk, helps prioritize supervisory resources and identifies zone-specific threats and control gaps. It is not mandatory, and jurisdictions without multiple FTZs may not need to use it.

## Assessment approach

The module generates a risk score for each FTZ by combining:

- Inherent-risk indicators.
- TBML control effectiveness.
- Other relevant mitigating factors.

The module uses a weighted-average model. It first calculates **inherent risk**, representing the risk before mitigation. It then calculates **residual risk** by reducing inherent risk according to the strength and effectiveness of mitigation measures. Stronger mitigation creates a larger reduction from inherent to residual risk, subject to the module’s assumptions.

The spreadsheet entry process is intentionally simple. The substantive work is the evidence-based justification of each rating, including the documentation of specific threats, risk factors, assumptions and supporting evidence.

## Customization and assumptions

An assumptions page is hidden by default. It can be revealed in Excel by right-clicking a worksheet tab.

Subject to clear justification and documentation, assessors may:

- Rename indicators.
- Remove indicators that are not relevant locally.
- Add locally relevant indicators.
- Adjust indicator weights.
- Choose linear or geometric modelling for the impact of mitigating factors.
- Set the proportion of inherent risk considered unmitigable, known as “rock-bottom” risk.

Any changes should be recorded in the assessment documentation so that results remain transparent, reproducible and comparable over time.

## Assessment page for each FTZ

Each zone should have an assessment page containing:

- The zone name and profile.
- Ratings for each inherent-risk factor.
- Ratings for each mitigation factor.
- Evidence and rationale for every rating.
- Specific threats and risk factors.
- Overall inherent-risk and residual-risk results.

Scenario-based entries in illustrative worksheets are not associated with any particular jurisdiction and should not be treated as default country or zone ratings.

## Inherent-risk factors

### 1. Trade volume

Assesses shipment counts, values and growth against available oversight resources. Persistently high or rapidly rising volumes that exceed control capacity can increase documentation errors, delayed checks and missed anomalies.

### 2. Complexity of supply chains and trade transactions

Assesses multi-leg routing, trans-shipment, split consignments, numerous intermediaries, related-party trading and re-exports with limited transformation. Complexity can obscure origin, goods, value and payment.

### 3. Transaction patterns

Examines trans-shipment intensity, re-exports with negligible processing, related-party trading, circular movements, minimal value addition and persistent anomalies without a commercial rationale.

### 4. Counterparty profile

Considers the jurisdictions trading with the FTZ. Large volumes involving high-risk or sanctioned jurisdictions can increase exposure to TBML and sanctions-related schemes.

### 5. Product prevalence and sector concentration

Assesses concentration in small, high-value or portable goods such as gold, jewellery, electronics, cigarettes and art, as well as other sectors with recognized TBML, intellectual-property or sanctions risks.

### 6. Corporate opacity

Assesses the transparency of beneficial ownership and control among FTZ operators, trade facilitators and reporting intermediaries. Special-purpose entities, nominees and offshore structures without verifiable owners increase risk.

### 7. Geography and logistics

Considers proximity to borders, conflict zones and high-risk corridors, as well as free-port features, long-term storage and high-value warehouses that may facilitate diversion, substitution or concealment.

### 8. Trade finance and payment channels

Assesses cash handling, informal value transfer, cryptocurrency use and settlement outside regulated banking. Weakly controlled channels reduce screening and auditability.

### 9. Crime context

Places the FTZ within the local illicit-trade economy. Relevant indicators include smuggling, counterfeit goods, intellectual-property crime, illegal mining, waste and wildlife trafficking, stolen vehicles and illicit pharmaceuticals.

## Risk-mitigation factors

### 1. Beneficial-ownership transparency

Assesses whether accurate, current and verified beneficial-ownership information exists and is accessible to FIUs, customs, tax authorities and supervisors.

### 2. AML/CFT monitoring and reporting

Evaluates entity- and shipment-level screening, anomaly detection and the quality of escalations to competent authorities.

### 3. AML/CFT supervision

Assesses the intensity and effectiveness of external supervision of financial intermediaries and designated non-financial businesses and professions operating in the FTZ, including examinations, remediation and sanctions.

### 4. Authorization, onboarding and periodic reviews

Evaluates the gateway for approving FTZ operators and tenants. It should verify legal existence, beneficial ownership, fitness and propriety, sanctions exposure, adverse information, business purpose and expected activity. Reviews should be risk-based, time-triggered and event-triggered.

### 5. Record-keeping

Assesses the retention, retrievability and tamper resistance of invoices, transport records, stock movements, ownership information and payments.

### 6. Customs controls and footprint

Considers customs presence, legal powers and operational access inside the FTZ, including the ability to detain goods, seize assets and query records.

### 7. Targeting and inspections

Evaluates data-driven targeting, unannounced checks and effective remedial action such as fines, licence suspension, tenant exit and referrals.

### 8. Physical security

Assesses perimeter controls, access management, CCTV, seal integrity and inventory reconciliation to prevent substitution or unauthorized removal of goods.

### 9. Staffing

Evaluates whether FTZ management has sufficient competent and stable personnel, role segregation and targeted training.

### 10. Digitalization

Assesses integration among FTZ platforms, national single windows, customs and FIUs, including the quality, immutability, interoperability and auditability of electronic records.

## Interpretation and outputs

Module 4 supports:

- Comparative ranking of FTZs by relative risk.
- Identification of zone-specific threats and vulnerabilities.
- Prioritization of inspections, supervision, training and investment.
- Targeted reforms and action planning.
- More granular support for Module 3.b and relevant parts of Modules 1 and 2.

Module 4 does not directly feed the master template for overall TBML risk. Where used, its findings may inform FTZ control, threat and vulnerability assessments, but the module remains complementary to the core assessment.

## Documentation requirements

For each FTZ and indicator, retain:

- The participants and responsible agencies.
- Data sources and documents reviewed.
- The rating and its rationale.
- Key assumptions, caveats and limitations.
- Specific threats and risk factors.
- Any changes to indicators, weights or model assumptions.
- Follow-up actions, owners and timelines.
