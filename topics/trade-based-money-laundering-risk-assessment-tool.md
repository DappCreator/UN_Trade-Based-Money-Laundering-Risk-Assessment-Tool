# Trade-Based Money Laundering Risk Assessment Tool

## Overview

The **UNODC–World Bank Trade-Based Money Laundering (TBML) Risk Assessment Tool** is a spreadsheet-based, evidence-driven framework for jurisdictions to identify, assess and mitigate money-laundering risks arising from cross-border trade in goods. It can be used alongside a broader national money-laundering risk assessment, as a supplementary module, or as an independent deep-dive assessment of trade-related laundering risks.

The accompanying **Self-Assessment Guidance Manual** explains the methodology, data requirements, scoring logic and implementation steps. It is designed for a multi-agency working group and supports the translation of assessment findings into risk-based policy, supervisory and operational actions.

## Objectives

1. Understand exposure to TBML threats in the mainland and free trade zones (FTZs).
2. Identify structural, sector-specific and institutional vulnerabilities.
3. Assess the quality and effectiveness of existing prevention, detection, investigation, prosecution and adjudication controls.
4. Develop a targeted action plan to reduce the jurisdiction's TBML risk posture.

## Tool structure

The tool has three interrelated modules, with separate analysis for the mainland and FTZs where applicable:

1. **Threat module** — evaluates exposure to TBML threats arising from international trade.
2. **Inherent vulnerability module** — identifies weaknesses linked to trade systems, sectors, institutions and FTZ operations.
3. **Control landscape module** — assesses the adequacy of mitigation measures and institutional capacity.

The outputs connect threat, vulnerability and controls to produce inherent and residual risk views, an overall risk assessment, an impact map and priority analysis.

## Recommended working group

A diverse working group of approximately 10–20 members is recommended. Participants should ideally represent:

- Customs authorities
- Financial intelligence units (FIUs)
- Law enforcement agencies
- Banking supervisors or financial-sector regulators
- Prosecution services
- Tax authorities
- Free-trade-zone authorities, where applicable

The multi-agency composition enables access to trade, financial and enforcement data and supports structured expert deliberation.

## Assessment cycle

The guidance manual follows a sequential process:

1. **Identify and collect data** — gather representative, validated and preferably disaggregated information using the assessment templates.
2. **Assess threats** — examine the jurisdiction's exposure to TBML threats in the mainland and FTZs.
3. **Assess vulnerabilities** — evaluate structural and sector-specific weaknesses.
4. **Analyse mitigating measures** — assess the control landscape and institutional effectiveness.
5. **Draw conclusions and plan actions** — prioritize practical reforms, capacity-building and inter-agency measures.

Input variables are scored using the manual's criteria. Scores may combine administrative data, quantitative analysis, expert input and structured consensus. Intermediate variables synthesize inputs into composite assessments before results are entered into the spreadsheet tool.

## Control landscape indicators

The control landscape includes indicators covering:

- National commitment, policy and coordination
- Domestic and international information-sharing
- Control of corruption
- Trade-facilitator awareness, integrity, oversight and record-keeping
- Reporting-intermediary supervision, sanctions, customer due diligence, monitoring and resources
- Customs IT monitoring, physical detection tools, staffing, skills and internal controls
- FIU integrity, skills, resources and analytical capacity
- Investigative, prosecutorial and judicial integrity, independence, skills, staffing and resources

The scoring scale shown in the material ranges from **very low (0.2)** through **low (0.3)**, **medium-low (0.4)**, **medium (0.5)**, **medium-high (0.6)**, **high (0.7)** and **very high (0.8)**. These values are illustrative assessment scores and should be applied according to the manual's criteria rather than copied as a default country profile.

## Outputs and use of results

The automatically generated **impact map** provides a visual overview of strengths and weaknesses across threats, vulnerabilities and controls. It helps jurisdictions identify urgent attention areas, improvement opportunities and controls that are already performing well.

Results can support:

- Risk-based policy and legislative planning
- Targeted supervision of trade and financial intermediaries
- Stronger customs, FIU, law-enforcement and tax cooperation
- Better integration and use of trade and financial data
- Enhanced oversight of free trade zones
- Targeted training and capacity-building
- Prioritized operational and institutional actions
